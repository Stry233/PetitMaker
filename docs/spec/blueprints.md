# Blueprints

This area covers Blueprint, the fifth mode of the row: saving a framed rectangle of the map, ground and objects together, as a named blueprint in a library kept in the browser, placing it again on any map, and moving blueprints between people as files.

## Mode and library

- **BLUE-01** The mode row offers Blueprint as its fifth tile, before Generate, in every arrangement; pressing it opens the Blueprint strip in the bar's place. A blueprint is never a category inside Objects or Generate, because it carries terrain. On an upright screen the tiles keep their size and never overlap; where the six do not fit between the margins the mode row scrolls sideways, and the open mode's tile is brought into view. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-02** The strip follows the Generate bar: tabs for All and each folder; a round New folder button beside the search (a field on a wide screen, a round button upright), where the Objects shelf has its brush button, which asks for the folder's name under itself; one scrolling row of blueprint cards in the library's own order (BLUE-10), showing a thumbnail and a name, led by a New blueprint card drawn as Generate's import card, a plus with its name, which starts a new blueprint and is all an empty library shows. Picking a card arms it with a chosen ring and nothing on its corner; with a mouse a right press on a card opens its menu (BLUE-10) and a double click on its name renames it; the row under the cards stays the row's scrollbar, with no action buttons. A press on the map places it once (BLUE-06), and Escape disarms. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner pick 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-03** The strip unfolds in place into a library explorer and folds back, the band and every piece both show travelling on one curve over one duration, drawn over the scrolling rows and the band's edge on the way so none of them is cut; a piece standing beside another in a row (the view switch beside the search, a folder name beside All) travels only with that one, crossing over between its two places, the unfold square stays on the band's top right corner and moves only as that corner does, and what only one shows fades where it stands, in late as the strip unfolds and out early as it folds. In the details list a card's picture travels to and from its row's picture from its own place in the card, never the whole row, and the card's plate and name travel with it, standing around the picture on every frame as they fade, out early as the card goes to a row and in late as it comes from one, while the row's words fade where they stand. Unfolded on a wide screen it covers the right-hand column and the assistant's seat, the character standing under it until it folds again, and the folder list starts at its top. The explorer's head row ends in one family of controls on the cream plate, of the search field's height with a mouse and in touch mode of the touch bars' fingertip size, the air between them grown to match, each with the app's one hover and press: in touch mode Select, a word on the plate that stands on the yellow plate as Done while it picks, New folder and Export as round buttons, the search (in touch mode a round search button that opens into the field over the controls before it, as the upright strip's does, and closes again clearing it), the grid and details switch on a cream track with the yellow plate, and a round fold button; the upright explorer's row of Select, Export and the switch takes the same sizes. Wide screens unfold it with a square, in touch mode also by pulling the band up, and the round fold button at the end of the explorer's head row folds it again, the unfolded band wearing no grip in either mode; portrait screens unfold it only with the seam handle, whose height the session keeps, and let go below the turn it rests as the strip. Escape leaves picking first, then folds. Unfolding it puts the map's selection aside and folding it gives that selection back, while leaving Blueprint mode or opening another map folds it with its selection and picking ending and gives nothing back; a press on the map that does more than move the view, and anything that makes a map selection while it stands (Ctrl+A, an Undo or Redo bringing one back, a placement), folds it first, ending its own selection, so the map's selection and the explorer's are never both live. Inside, a press selects one blueprint; with a mouse Ctrl toggles one, Shift extends from the last pressed and a drag over empty space draws a selection box; in touch mode Select shows tick circles for picking several and becomes Done. A double press folds the explorer and arms that blueprint. No status line or action buttons stand in it: a card is handled directly (BLUE-10). A carried card rises off its row as an object card does, and over the strip or the explorer a copy of it follows the pointer, at a strip card's size wherever it was carried from, growing from the size its entry drew it at as it rises; a row of the details list rises in place as a card does, its picture lifting and its plate lightening, and the card follows the pointer, held by its middle, until the release settles the row back; a card carried from a selection of several carries them all as one stack wearing their count: the copies of those in view first travel from their own slots to the pointer, those scrolled out of view joining there, and only then does the stack form, its count appearing with it, while the selected cards' slots keep the carried look; over a folder or the delete target the copy shrinks about the pointer to show it drops in there, and grows back as it leaves; a card or stack dragged onto a folder moves there with a toast naming it; carried over the cards of the strip, the grid or the details list, the others move aside to open the slot it would take, and a release there moves it to that place in the library's order, without a toast: each card it carries sets off from where the copy stands under the pointer at the copy's size, never from its old slot, and travels into its new place on one decelerating curve, growing or shrinking to it, while the others finish making room; a row's picture sets off from the copy's picture, and where the copy shows more than what lands (a card around a row's picture, a stack) it travels around what lands as it fades out; while anything is carried the unfold square, or in the explorer the fold button, crossfades where it stands at its own size into the delete target, a pale red plate with a red glyph that turns red with a white glyph and grows while the carry is over it, and back as the carry ends (upright, where no square stands, the delete target crossfades in place of New folder and search at the head's end), and a drop there deletes; a single card carried above the panel's top makes the panel step aside so the map is free, and places on release; leaving Blueprint mode ends a carry at once, placing nothing, unfolding nothing and giving back no selection. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner pick 2026-11-05.

## Saving

- **BLUE-04** The New blueprint card and the selection card's Save as blueprint frame a rectangle with the shared region brush, rectangle tool only, which stops growing at 32 by 32 cells in either direction, so a larger frame cannot be drawn; the same brush in Generate and the assistant's region selection keeps no such limit. A frame touching a landmark or holding nothing, or a selection's frame larger than 32 by 32 cells, cannot be confirmed, and the region bar says why on a line of its own; New blueprint pressed in the open explorer first folds it on its own fold and then hands over to the region bar as the strip does. Done opens the save step, and one Cancel or Escape goes back to where the step was started: the strip, or the explorer, the strip coming back first and then unfolding again; any region painted for Generate stays as it was. A frame captures every terrain cell with its elevation and corner cuts, water, and every object whose whole footprint lies inside; objects straddling the edge, landmarks and notes are left out. Starting the frame step clears the selection (EDIT-25); Save as blueprint first takes its starting frame from it. Cancel or Escape back to where the step was started, also after stepping back from the save step, gives the selection back; saving, another mode or another map does not. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner ruling 2026-11-06; owner report 2026-11-06.
- **BLUE-05** The save step is a dock on the window plate floating over the bottom of the map in the bar's place, centred, clear of the mode row upright, with no window. It asks for a name (left empty, a numbered default) and an author (the last one used by default, with the anonymous switch inside the author field, which greys the field, still showing the author typed, until switched off; where the platform forbids names a player makes up, as Bilibili Toy does, the field takes no typing and the author is the player's nickname there, which turning the switch off asks the platform for (the platform asks the player's consent the first time), the switch staying on when none comes) in one field style, and for a folder (by default the one open in the strip when it holds the player's own blueprints, My Blueprints or a folder the player named, else My Blueprints) in the app's dropdown, and shows the 2D and the 3D picture as two of the strip's blueprint cards on light plates, their names in the plate's ink, the map's current view chosen. A viewfinder at the pictures' shape is outlined over the map above the dock, the map around it dimmed under the interface, which stays undimmed; the dim comes and goes with the dock. While the step stands both views show only what the frame holds, laid where it was framed on the empty map of the template, as the pictures draw it: neighbours, objects straddling the frame's edge, landmarks, notes, grid lines, chunk bounds and layer numbers are hidden, their choices in Settings staying as they are, a press on the map only moves the view, and the open map, its history and its selection stay as they are, no key, rail button or gesture editing them, while anything else that leaves the frame nothing to save takes the step back to the frame step on it; leaving the step shows the open map again at once, the view where it was and the grid, chunk bounds and layer numbers as Settings choose. While the step stands the interface cannot be hidden: the rail's Hide interface button stands unavailable until the step ends. Each view is first shown with the frame filling the viewfinder, and again after the screen is turned; the viewfinder stays put while the map moves or turns under it, the pictures show what it frames, live, each framed exactly as its view shows the map inside the viewfinder, the 2D one turned as the 2D view is and the 3D one seen from where the camera stands, a press on the other card switches the map's view, and saving stores each picture as it was last framed. The strip and library show the one matching the map's current view. When the frame holds another author's placement the dock names them as Adapted from. Cancel or Escape goes back to the frame step on the same frame; another map or mode ends the step. Saving arms the new blueprint on its folder's tab; while a save lands Cancel, Escape and Save do nothing, and one landing after another mode or map ended the step keeps the blueprint but arms nothing and opens no folder; a save the browser refuses says so and leaves the dock open. Names and authors go through the text review. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner pick 2026-11-06; owner review 2026-11-06; owner request 2026-11-06; owner pick 2026-11-07.

## Placing

- **BLUE-06** A placement never removes anything already on the map and changes existing terrain only by raising it or by turning an uncut top block into the blueprint's water at the same height (BLUE-08): each part (terrain cell, corner cut, object) lands only where the live rules accept it beside what is there, and parts that cannot land are skipped. When anything was skipped one toast says how many parts did not place and the reason that left out most of them; a placement that skipped nothing says nothing. One placement is one undo step. A press on the map with a card picked, or a card dragged onto the map, places once and puts the blueprint down, as an item placed up to its limit is: nothing stays armed, no ghost follows the pointer, and in every input mode the placement stays a group (BLUE-07) and is selected whole. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner review 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-07** Every placement stays a group of what it laid, its objects and the terrain cells it built. In Blueprint mode a group is hovered and selected as a whole, as a house is: the pointer anywhere in its frame, or on any of its objects or terrain cells, outlines its frame and a click there selects all of it, over open ground and over objects in the frame that are not its own, the one placed last winning where frames overlap, the selection card named after the blueprint, its round picture a dashed square, and the marks outlining its frame; the card's details list the group's objects by kind (picture, name and count, a press narrowing the selection to those objects), then a Mountain row and a Water row counting its terrain cells with each terrain's colour, each only when the group holds some; the selection comes before any frame: the pointer on a selected object answers for the selection, so while that is objects rather than a whole group, as after that narrowing, it outlines that object alone and a press there acts on the selection as outside Blueprint mode, a drag moving the selected objects and leaving the rest of the group where it stands, while elsewhere in the frame it still hovers and selects the group whole; outside Blueprint mode clicks select single objects and cells as before, and leaving Blueprint mode puts down a placement selected whole, as no undo step, so no key outside the mode turns or deletes part of it. The selection card's Rotate, or E, turns a selected group a quarter about its centre, its Flip, or F, mirrors it about its centre (BLUE-14), and a drag from any of its objects or any cell of its frame moves it, each as one undo step of its own, even right after the placement: its undo gives the group back where it stood, whole and selected as before it (EDIT-72), and redo repeats the steps in order; its ghost is the group's own, what no longer lands is skipped and said, earlier work is never touched, and a move, turn or flip where nothing of it would land is refused with the group left as it was; Delete, by its key or on the selection card, removes exactly what the group still holds at once, with no question however little that is, setting each terrain cell back to what it held before the placement where the rules allow, as an edit of its own, and undoing it brings the group back, not selected. A group keeps what is left of it: an object of it deleted, moved or turned, or a terrain cell of it cut or repainted, leaves the group for good, even when later repainted exactly as laid, and what remains is still the group; nothing added inside its frame later joins it, and a group with nothing left is gone. A group also keeps the blueprint it was placed from, turned and flipped as the group stands: a turn, flip or move lays that blueprint again less what has left the group, so a part a placement, turn, flip or move skipped lands wherever it now fits, and what still does not land is skipped and said. Copy, Cut, Paste and a Ctrl+drag take a group selected whole with its terrain and what it would lay now, the parts it skipped included (EDIT-53, EDIT-54, EDIT-57, EDIT-60, EDIT-61): the copy lands as placing the blueprint does, its terrain relative to the surface under it and only raising it, what cannot land skipped and said (BLUE-06, BLUE-08), and becomes a new group of the same blueprint with the same name and Adapted from, as placing it again from the library would; a name or original author still waiting for the background text review waits in the copy too. With the selection narrowed to some of its objects, Copy and a Ctrl+drag take those objects alone, with no terrain and no group. Undo and redo bring a group back as they bring back what it laid. Map files and the autosave keep groups with the parts each skipped, so a reopened map still selects them whole and a turn, flip, move or copy there still lays what was skipped, while a group from a file written before groups kept those lays only what it holds; a group's name and original author that a map file brings pass the background text review first, the card meanwhile naming the group as a numbered blueprint and a frame over it naming no Adapted from; share codes, share images and the map's content as the game reads it never carry them. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner pick 2026-11-05; owner report 2026-11-07; owner pick 2026-11-07; owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **BLUE-08** Terrain is relative to the surface it is dropped on, never to the layer panel: the blueprint's lowest ground lands on the surface height most of the cells under its frame stand at (the lower on a tie), its rises stack on that surface, and its objects stand at that height plus their own rise; while a blueprint is armed the layer panel highlights that surface. A blueprint cell with no terrain leaves its target cell alone, unless the cell is lower than that surface and the blueprint needs it there, when it is filled up to it: under one of the blueprint's objects, as the rim or floor of the blueprint's water, or as the support its terrain stands on under the game's rules. Existing terrain is only ever raised, never lowered, cut or reshaped, with one exception: blueprint water turns an uncut top block whose top is the water's own height into water at that height, as the game converts a block, and never digs lower; water standing above an uncut top block raises that block to the water's height, as the blueprint's mountains raise what they stand on, with the water as its new top. A target cell is skipped, and the ghost marks it, when an object stands on it, when it or a neighbour carries an edge cut, when it is higher than the blueprint needs, when it lies outside the buildable zone, or when water would replace any other terrain. Layers past the game's height limit are skipped. When a skipped part leaves the rest of the blueprint breaking the game's rules, as a waterfall missing a cap does, the rest still lands as far as it stands legal: the cells nearest the skipped part give way a step at a time, water to the block at its own height and a block to one layer lower, until the rest stands. The ghost marks exactly the cells the placement skips or lays short of the blueprint, and shows the terrain a placement builds as blocks at the height each cell lands at, with the blueprint's objects standing on them (UI-69). Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner report 2026-11-07.
- **BLUE-09** While a blueprint is carried or armed, E turns it a quarter turn clockwise, as it turns objects, and F flips it (BLUE-14): arming or carrying one, even the one already armed, or coming back to Blueprint mode with one armed, clears the selection, so a placement selected before stays as it is, and a carry that never lands gives that selection back; once placed, the placement stays selected in every input mode and the selection card's Rotate, or E, turns it whole and its Flip, or F, flips it whole (BLUE-07). Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner review 2026-11-05; owner report 2026-11-06.
- **BLUE-14** Flip mirrors what it acts on, each press moving it to the next of four orientations as the screen shows them: mirrored left to right, then top to bottom, then both, which is a half turn, then back as it was. The selection card shows it beside Rotate for a selection of two or more objects and for a placed blueprint selected whole, as the touch multi-select bar does while two or more objects are selected, and F, its key, does the same there and on a blueprint armed or carried before it is placed, as E turns one (BLUE-09), whose ghost then shows it flipped; in touch mode a blueprint is placed first and flipped from the card, and a single object has no Flip, only Rotate. A selection flips about the centre of its bounding box, the cycle starting again whenever the selection changes, and lands every member or none, as a turn does (EDIT-29); a placed group flips about its frame's centre and stays the same group, with the same name, author and Adapted from, laid as a turn lays it: each part where the rules accept it, what cannot land skipped and said, nothing else on the map removed, and a flip where nothing of it would land refused with the group left as it was. The game never mirrors an item's art, so a flip mirrors where each object stands and turns its facing to the mirrored side, a door, a ramp's downhill side and a print's exits among them, while a bridge keeps the axis it spans; it mirrors the terrain cells, their corner cuts and fillets and a road's corner cuts, and every item keeps its own look. A press on a selection or a placed group plays a short flip in both views where a turn plays its arc, like a card turning over, squashing along the flip's axis and opening out again a little past flat before it settles, on the same timing as a turn, which steps straight to the result under reduced motion; each press is one undo step of its own, its undo giving back what stood before it and redo the press again. A flipped group keeps its blueprint flipped as it stands, so a later turn, move or copy lays it flipped, also after the map is reopened or restored, and keeps its place in the cycle for the session, which a placement takes from the blueprint armed and a copy starts afresh; map files and the autosave keep no place in the cycle, so a group reopened, reloaded or restored counts as it stands as its start and its next press mirrors it left to right. Source: owner request 2026-11-07; owner pick 2026-11-07.

## Library and files

- **BLUE-10** The library lives in the browser, in flat folders, and survives reloads and planet changes; it can be searched by name whatever the case, accents and character width, shown as a grid or details list, and blueprints can be multi-selected. A browser slow to open the library shows it once it opens, saves made meanwhile landing then, and only a browser that refuses it says it could not save. In the open explorer a switch between the grid and the details list carries each card's picture between its place in the grid card and its row's picture on one decelerating curve, the card's plate and name travelling around the picture as they fade, while the rows' words and what only the grid shows fade where they stand, in late and out early; opening another folder or searching carries the cards both show to their new places, those leaving fading out early and those arriving fading in late, while the folders, the title and the count change in place. It keeps one order of its own, which All, every folder, the strip and the grid always show: newest saved first until a card or stack is dragged to another place among the cards (BLUE-03), a move inside a folder or a search placing it beside its neighbours there, and a new or imported blueprint going first. The order survives reloads, Settings, Local data wipes it with the rest, and no file carries it. The details list's Name and Saved heads sort the list; dragging a row while it is sorted drops the sort and shows the library's order. With a mouse no card wears a button: a right press opens its menu and a double click on its name opens the name card. In touch mode, in the open explorer, the one selected blueprint (never several, and none while tick circles pick) wears a round menu button on its card's corner or at its details row's end, named on a hold, which opens that blueprint's menu hanging from it, and a press on it while the menu stands open puts the menu away; the strip's cards wear none. Dragging a card or the selected stack onto a folder moves it; dropping it on the delete target deletes it at once, with a toast naming the blueprint or counting several and offering Undo, which puts them back as they were. A folder's own menu, opened by a right press or a hold on its tab or entry (never on All), renames, exports or deletes it, offering only what that folder allows: Export only while it holds a blueprint and the edition writes files (BLUE-11), no Rename or Delete on My Blueprints, which opens no menu while it holds nothing, and no Delete on the Examples folder; a mouse's right press on a card or a details row opens the menu of that entry, or of the selection it is in, which offers Rename first for a single blueprint, opening the name card, then Move to, moving it to a folder (or none) from a list, then Export, exporting them as one file (BLUE-11), and Delete, deleting it as a drop on the delete target does, with the same Undo; in touch mode only the menu button opens it: a finger's hold lifts the entry, and a finger moves and deletes several by dragging; several selected entries wear only the selection's look, and their count shows only on a carried stack; in the open explorer the Delete key deletes the selection as a drop on the delete target does, with the same Undo. Each delete's toast keeps its own Undo, and plain notices leave the band before it does. A folder rename keeps the folder open and is refused, with the reason in the card, for a blank name or another folder's name; deleting a folder first asks, naming how many blueprints go with it, then removes it and them and opens All. Settings, Local data wipes it with everything else. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05; owner pick 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-11** The explorer's head Export, a round button, exports what the explorer shows as one JSON file: the selected blueprints when any are (one or several), else what the search found, else the open folder, else the whole library; its name, shown as round buttons show theirs, says exactly what a press exports, and it stands dimmed when that is nothing. A folder's menu exports that folder, and a blueprint's or a selection's menu (BLUE-10) those blueprints, as the same kind of file; nothing else in Blueprint exports, the edition that writes no files offers none of them, and every export writes its file only once the export notice every export opens is accepted. A file holds names, authors and folders only as they are shown, newest saved first whatever the library's order: a file of several blueprints names each one's own folder whatever was exported, but none for one in My Blueprints or in no folder, and a file of one blueprint names no folder; they import from the library's Import, the app's Import entry and its drop zone. Each blueprint lands in the folder its file names for it, made if missing, so a file recreates its folders; an older file naming one folder for all its blueprints lands them there, and one naming none in the folder open when the file was dropped, or in none when that folder is gone by the time it lands. A blueprint whose file names no folder, or My Blueprints as any of the seven languages shows it, lands in its author's folder, named after the author in the interface language (“Mei's Blueprints”) with the author's name as written, made if missing: an anonymous one in Anonymous Blueprints, and one by the author the save step offers (BLUE-05) in My Blueprints; a folder name that reads in any language as an author's folder or Anonymous Blueprints lands in that folder, never in a new folder of that text. An author's folder's name follows the interface language live, and otherwise it is an ordinary folder; one the library already holds unchanged is skipped with a toast; a file naming catalog items the catalog lacks opens with those parts skipped and the toast counting them; older files open in newer releases. An imported file opens Blueprint on the folder it all landed in when the library already shows that folder (on All when it landed in several), else on All and then on that folder once the review approves its name, unless another folder was chosen while it was imported or reviewed, which stays open, and a later older file without a folder never lands in the open folder while that waits for review; an imported blueprint's pictures are drawn by the app, never taken from the file, and a blueprint imported or saved where none could be drawn gets them once a map is open; a damaged or newer file is refused with an Import message, and an imported name, author and folder show only after the background text review approves them: meanwhile the name reads as a numbered default, numbered in the order the library's blueprints were saved so it keeps its number as more are saved, and the author and folder as none, an author's folder standing nowhere until its author's name is approved, and a refused one is replaced for good, a blueprint whose author's name is refused staying anonymous in no folder. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-12** A blueprint names its author, or Anonymous, and at most one Adapted from, the original author. A new frame that overlaps placements of others' blueprints on the open map, while anything of them stands (BLUE-07), names the original author of the one it overlaps most, automatically, and it cannot be removed, though the save step shows it only while the author entered is someone else, as the save keeps it; one's own and anonymous blueprints name no one, undoing a placement forgets it until it is redone, and a map file reopened remembers its placements; an adaptation of an adaptation keeps the first original, and a frame without others' placements has none. Source: owner ruling 2026-11-05.
- **BLUE-13** Every library holds a folder named My Blueprints, the player's own, first among the folders: empty on a new library, given once to a library that had none with every blueprint left where it was, and never renamed or deleted. The first time the library loads in a browser it also holds a folder named Examples, holding a few example blueprints by PetitMaker that together carry mountain, water and objects. The name of My Blueprints, the Examples folder's and each example's follow the interface language live wherever they show (the strip, the explorer, the details list, menus, toasts, search, files exported and the selection card of a placed example) until the player renames the Examples folder or an example, whose own text then stays as typed in every language; otherwise the examples are ordinary blueprints, moved, arranged and deleted as the player's own, and a deleted example never comes back, while the Examples folder, renamed or not, is never deleted. No other folder can take a name My Blueprints, the Examples folder or an author's folder (BLUE-11) shows, a file exported from them carries the names as shown in the current language, and an imported file never takes the app's own names, whatever it holds. After Settings, Local data the next load gives My Blueprints and the examples again. Every example is at most 32 by 32 cells and places on open flat ground at any turn with nothing skipped; their names, author and folder are the app's own and never wait for the background text review, and their pictures are drawn by the app as an imported blueprint's are (BLUE-11). Source: owner ruling 2026-11-06.
