# Interface

This area covers how the editor speaks, moves and responds: its seven languages and their copy, motion and reduced motion, how controls behave and ask for confirmation, map navigation and keyboard shortcuts, the text check, Help, and how the editor keeps working when a part of it, the browser or the device falls short.

## Languages and copy

- **UI-01** The interface and Help are complete in seven languages (Chinese, English, Japanese, Russian, Thai, Indonesian and French): every string exists in each, none is blank, and each carries the same placeholders as English.
- **UI-02** On a first visit the interface language follows the browser's language when it is one of the seven, and English otherwise; a chosen language is remembered and applied before the editor first appears.
- **UI-03** Switching the language updates every visible text at once, including notices already on screen, without a reload.
- **UI-04** Help and the welcome tips name each control with the exact label that control shows in the same language.
- **UI-05** Copy in every language reads as plain, native, professional writing; no interface text uses em dashes or dot separators, and only error toasts take the form of a category followed by a reason.
- **UI-06** Counts take the grammatical form each language needs, numbers of five or more digits are grouped the local way, and seeds always show exactly as typed.

## Motion

- **UI-07** Every state change in the interface animates. Source: owner ruling 2026-08-29.
- **UI-08** Every animation respects reduced motion: Settings offers Auto, Reduced and Full, Auto follows the device setting, and under reduced motion decorative motion stops while progress and errors step straight to their new state. Source: owner ruling 2026-08-29.
- **UI-39** At every interface scale, an element that glides to a new place starts where it stood and ends where it lands, without a jump at either end.
- **UI-59** A choice drawn as a row of segments, such as Save and share's Keep working, For a friend and Into the game, marks the chosen segment with a plate centred on it, at every interface scale, in either input mode and on an upright or a wide screen; choosing another segment slides the plate to it, where it stops without passing it or swinging back, and it stays on its segment while the window holding the choice resizes. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.

## Controls and confirmations

- **UI-09** A control never changes itself to ask for confirmation; a confirmation unrolls as its own surface from the control, or the action runs at once and can be undone. Source: owner ruling 2026-09-28.
- **UI-10** Controls keep their positions when state changes: an unavailable control dims in place instead of disappearing, a button whose action is running keeps its size and colour and shows loading dots in place of its label under the busy pointer, and a control two tools share stays where it is when the user switches between them.
- **UI-11** Labels never shrink to fit; a label that lacks room wraps, scrolls or ends in an ellipsis with its full text available. The layer control's name of the layer being built on always shows whole, its outline included, in every language, at every layer and at every interface scale. Source: owner report 2026-10-29.
- **UI-43** A row that arrives or unfolds in a list takes the room its content needs once it has settled, so a row whose words or result grow afterwards, or wrap in a narrow panel or a long language, pushes the rows below it down and never draws over them.
- **UI-44** Installed as an app, the editor turns with the screen, upright or wide, and enters immersive mode at the first tap or key after it opens or returns to the screen, unless the user left immersive mode with the Settings button, which keeps it off until that button enters it again; leaving with the system's own gesture holds only until the app is next brought back. Source: owner report 2026-10-27.
- **UI-12** Escape closes only the surface in front, and a press outside a dismissible surface closes it without also acting on what lies beneath, except that in touch mode a tap outside a hint bubble closes the bubble and still acts where it lands while no other such surface is open.
- **UI-66** Every open window carries exactly one title heading, which names the window to assistive technology: the title in its head, or in Help the title of the page shown, or Help's own title while its page list stands alone.
- **UI-41** The layer panel shows every layer at each of its sizes, on an upright or a wide window, in either input mode and at every interface scale: its largest size lays the layers in as many columns as its room holds whole, up to its usual square, a layer's name ending in an ellipsis with its full text rather than widening its tile, and where the layers stand deeper than the room, the panel's head and notes row stay at its top while the layers scroll beneath them as one region with an edge fade. Source: owner request 2026-10-26.

## Map navigation and shortcuts

- **UI-13** The mouse wheel over the map zooms it, in steps for a mouse wheel and smoothly for a trackpad pinch or a two-finger touch. Source: owner ruling 2026-08-29.
- **UI-14** A horizontal list that overflows scrolls sideways under the mouse wheel and the trackpad's sideways and vertical gestures, and the wheel over it never reaches the map; over a list that fits, the map receives it. A sideways gesture always scrolls the list; a vertical one scrolls it sideways only while nothing else there scrolls vertically (the list itself, or a page or window body around it, as with a wide table in Help), and otherwise scrolls that instead. At either end the list stays put and the wheel goes no further; a pinch (ctrl+wheel) always passes through to its own zoom, and touch scrolls lists as before. Source: owner request 2026-10-25.
- **UI-35** In the 3D view a two-finger twist turns the ground with the fingers, as a map app does: fingers turning clockwise on screen turn the map clockwise on screen. In touch mode the view controls on the right offer no turn buttons, nor zoom buttons, since the twist and a pinch do both, and the column closes up without their gap; with a mouse the 3D view shows both turn buttons. Source: owner report 2026-10-24; owner request 2026-10-24.
- **UI-15** Every keyboard shortcut can be rebound, and binding a key already in use moves it from its previous command, which is left unbound.
- **UI-16** Map shortcuts do nothing while a window is open.

## Text review

- **UI-17** The map title, description, footer and Letter text reach a picture, preview, generation or export only after the text check approves that exact text; an edit, a pending check, a refusal or a check that could not finish keeps it out.
- **UI-18** When text cannot be approved, the user can export without added text, which leaves out the title and description and uses the default footer while keeping the draft for later editing.
- **UI-19** The text check runs on the device and never uploads the text. It saves no verdict on export text or custom tags, and keeps only one on a custom planet's name: the planet library marks an approved name as checked and replaces a refused one with the default name (FILE-32). Text the user typed stays where the editor already keeps it, custom tags in the autosave and the footer template in the export settings.
- **UI-20** A confirmed finding from the on-device check of the map picture disables the export controls, while a check that fails or times out never counts as a finding. On a slow or busy device the check runs to its end for as long as it keeps making progress, and reports that it could not finish only when it stops progressing or fails.

## Help

- **UI-21** Every window has a Help topic, and in What's this? mode picking any control or window opens Help at its topic, while picking the bare map opens the topic for the tool in hand. The mode's banner stands within the window's side margins, its words wrapping in a long language and its exit dropping under them, as the held name of a glyph control does within the window. The name of the control under the pointer stands whole inside the window, over the control where there is room and under it otherwise.
- **UI-38** Help's pictures of the interface change nothing in the live editor: opening, reading or closing a Help page leaves the live assistant's character in the pose and dream the live panel gives her, with no gesture played on her.
- **UI-60** Help shows the interface in the reader's form: its pictures of the interface lay out the arrangement the reader's screen takes, upright or wide, at the reader's input mode; its demonstrations draw a fingertip in touch mode and the mouse cursor in PC mode, show keys only in PC mode and perform a gesture the touch way where it differs by finger; its key tables are left out in touch mode; and a page about touch mode or the upright layout pictures that form whichever the reader uses. Switching the input mode or turning the screen while Help is open changes its pictures and words at once. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.
- **UI-61** Where a Help section explains exactly what a welcome tips demonstration shows, it plays that demonstration under the tips' own rules (UI-49): the recording of the reader's language and form, its bars and captions, loading only as it nears the view, and resting on its picture under reduced motion or while the browser saves data; Lite, which carries no recordings, shows the section's live figure instead. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.
- **UI-62** A Help figure keeps its place: its box holds its size and stays centred in its column through its whole loop, a caption, notice or key changing length moving nothing around it, and each loop ends as it began, so the restart shows no jump. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.
- **UI-63** A Help figure draws the interface at the size the reader's own screen draws it, shrunk to fit its column by at most a quarter; where that cannot fit, it shows the part being explained at that live size instead of shrinking, and nothing in it stands outside its box. A demonstration's map frames what its scene shows (the ground it works on, where the pointer goes and its notes as drawn, labels included) centred and whole for the whole loop, closing in on a small subject up to a little past the scene's own scale and shrinking for a wide one in a narrow column, the controls over it keeping their size; a demonstration plays on a lived-in map, and its caption stands above the map and never covers it. Only the picture of the whole window at a glance, and of the tips over it, is a miniature by intent, its parts named above it in Help's own type. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.
- **UI-64** Everything a Help figure shows of the editor is the editor's own: its components and renderer on posed or sandboxed state, its tools making every stroke, note, selection and placement, and its own controls on the figure's map (curve handles, maze marks, the load meter, the selection card where the editor stands it, touch multi-select's plate and row while it is on, the bars and shelves), never a drawing of them; a demonstrated press on one of them lands on the control whose action it runs. A figure's presses on its own pictured controls never change the reader's input mode. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.
- **UI-65** A Help demonstration of building plays inside a lived-in map: around the plot it builds on, the planet stands built out as Generate builds it, with its ground, beach, roads, houses and trees, while the plot itself is the planet's own ground with nothing on it, and every visit shows the same neighbourhood. Source: owner request 2026-10-30.

## Resilience, devices and editions

- **UI-22** When one part of the interface fails to render, it shows a notice with a retry in its place while the map and every other part keep working; a part that fails to download offers a reload.
- **UI-23** A browser that cannot run the editor shows a notice in the visitor's language stating the requirement and offering download links, instead of a blank page.
- **UI-24** On a device without WebGL2 the editor works in full in 2D: the 3D switch shows as unavailable, the welcome tips leave out their 3D card and exports leave out 3D shots.
- **UI-25** The Lite edition runs fully offline from one package, with no network requests, external links or background workers, and keeps the shared editor, map rules, seven languages, and Planet and Maze generation. It leaves out the assistant, Letter, Picture and illustration features.
- **UI-26** The editor uses its touch mode while the last deliberate input was a finger or a pen and its PC mode while it was a mouse, a trackpad or a keyboard shortcut; it starts in touch mode only on a device whose only pointer is coarse.
- **UI-27** The mode never switches while a pointer is down, the press that causes a switch still acts on the control it was pressed on, and when it switches the interface re-sizes to the new mode's target size in one motion: the character, the corner actions, the right-hand column button by button, the mode tiles, the open bar or panel, the open assistant, the notices and an open window travel and resize from where they stood to where the new mode puts them, ending exactly there, an assistant the switching press opens included, its content standing inside its own panel once the motion ends; a window or the welcome tips' card whose content re-flows takes its outline from its old box to its new one exactly, its content scaled evenly inside and cut off at the outline on the way; content that the new mode lays out otherwise inside its window, card, bar or panel (a bar's controls, a window's rows, the welcome tips' reel for the other input) fades out where it stood as its new layout fades in, so no part of it is seen to jump, a picture, a map preview or a playing reel showing what it showed, and nothing in it fading twice; what only one mode shows fades out where it stood or in where it stands over the same motion, inside its window, card or bar where it is part of one (Settings' cursor and keyboard sections, the welcome tips' keyboard keys, the cards a shelf row has room for and the length of its scroll thumb); the map resizes once, and with reduced motion the interface re-sizes at once. In touch mode a notice, menu or window standing over the map keeps one proportion to the frame at every scale (UI-34), and a menu opened from a window or panel draws at that surface's size.
- **UI-28** An upright screen shows the portrait arrangement: the mode tiles in a bottom bar, the open mode's panel rising from the bar, the corner actions at the top right, and the layer control, undo and redo on the right, kept from the window's top, left and right by the same margins as the wide arrangement with a mouse and in touch mode by one smaller margin on both sides and a smaller top, so the map has more room; the side margins narrow alike only where the top row, or the mode tiles at the wide arrangement's spacing, would not fit between them, and every surface keeps the narrowed margins together: the top row, the right-hand column, the mode tiles, the open panel's content and the notices under the top row, each row of the open panel running to both margins rather than stopping short for the right-hand column; the top margin narrows only where an open panel would otherwise not fit. The bar and the open panel are one surface, dark under objects and generation and cream under the terrain tools, a shade deeper than the cream buttons standing on it; with no panel open the bar draws no surface and the tiles stand on the map. Each mode's panel stands as tall as everything it holds, so it neither scrolls nor fades at its top or foot, a docked assistant yielding to it (UI-32); the object panel's grid of cards, a list, scrolls inside it. While the bar's surface grows or shrinks, whether a panel grows, opens, closes or gives way to another mode's, nothing a panel holds, arriving or leaving, stands above that surface. Every upright panel spaces its rows alike, the same gap from one row's last mark or caption to the next row and a smaller one from the panel's last row to the mode tiles. A terrain panel stands its tool row lowest, so arming another tool never moves it, the size slider at that row's far end, always in that row: where room is short the slider narrows first, down to stops half its knob apart, and then the tools scroll sideways; when its room changes, the slider grows or shrinks toward its left with its right end standing still and the knob keeping its place on the track, following a glide of the interface's size as it runs and taking any other change in one motion (at once with reduced motion), and a tool row that fits before and after the change shows no edge fade during it, and the armed tool's other settings above in one row that scrolls sideways. The object panel's search button grows in place along the names' row into the search field, its words centred, the names, the selected one's mark and the brush button left of it (EDIT-64) fading out under it and taking no presses, and shrinks back when closed as they fade in. On a wide screen the object shelf's names scroll sideways when they overflow; only where the brush button and the whole search field would leave the names less room than two of them need whole does the field fold to the same search button at the row's end, the brush button left of it, growing over the row into the field and shrinking back in the same way, a search already typed staying open. When the room changes across that point the field shrinks into the button, or grows back out of it, in one motion (at once with reduced motion), and it unfolds only once the row has a little more room than it folded at, so a width near that point never switches back and forth. The object panel's cards stand one fixed gap apart, across the panel's width between its margins in as many columns as bring them nearest their own size, the cards taking any width left over rather than the gaps. They stand in a grid that scrolls down, or, where the grid has room for less than one row with its names, the gap and the next row's cards whole, in one row that scrolls sideways with the same spacing, turning between the two as the room changes, while the handle below is held as soon as the height it draws passes that one, a little past it either way so a finger resting there never turns them back and forth, letting go keeping the arrangement they stand in: each card travels and resizes from its place in the one arrangement to its place in the other while the panel takes its new height, moving with the panel a held handle draws and, however quickly the finger turns them back and forth, setting off each time from where it is drawn, ending exactly where the new arrangement puts it, a card that was out of view fades in, and the first card in view before the turn leads the view after it, at the grid's top left or the row's start, clear of the fade at that edge; with reduced motion the cards turn at once. A card carried from it keeps the arrangement the carry began in until it is let go. A handle on the object panel's top edge sets how tall its grid of cards stands, following the finger while held, between one row and a height that leaves only the top row clear, and the panel keeps that height while the page stays open; in touch mode a fingertip's press around its grip takes the handle, reaching above the panel's edge onto the map except where the top row stands there, and never over the names under it; a height too short for the grid is never kept at rest: letting go there, opening the panel, or a change of window, scale, arrangement or docked assistant that leaves the panel such a height settles it at the least height its one row needs, gliding there with the panel's other height changes (at once with reduced motion); an upright panel stands over the right-hand column, so a panel drawn that tall covers the column and the map rather than moving them, and lifting a card from it folds it to the line that cancels the carry, which returns it to its height when the carry ends. Arming an item folds the object panel to that item so the map is free, and pressing it opens the panel again. The welcome-back offer, generation's scope screen and a bar that failed to show stand on the same surface in the dark plate; the offer shows the saved map's picture across the panel's width, then its title, its words and both answers in one row, with a rule between it and the mode tiles. The arrival notice stands under the top row as one row (the planet, its words, OK and Switch planet) at its content's width, centred and within the frame's side margins, its words never taking a second line: when one line gives way to the next, the old fading out as the new fades in, the notice glides to the new line's width, at once with reduced motion, and a planet name or line too long for it ends in an ellipsis rather than wrapping. The mode tiles keep their places when a mode opens or closes, and a software keyboard opening does not change the arrangement. A touch device that reports how it is held follows that, a field with focus included; any other window takes the upright arrangement once it is clearly taller than wide and returns once it is clearly wider than tall, so a resize near square keeps the arrangement it has. When the arrangement changes, the character, the corner actions, the right-hand column, the mode tiles, the open panel with its bar, the open assistant and the notices travel and resize from where they stood to where the new arrangement puts them, in one motion that ends exactly there, the character so too where the change docks the open assistant elsewhere, never leaving her seat to pop in at the new one, and the open assistant travelling over the map rather than under it, and what only one arrangement has fades out or in; an open window, the welcome tips among them, keeps all it holds inside its edges on the way, the controls at its foot as well as its head; the map resizes once, a resize that keeps the arrangement moves nothing that way, the corner actions and the right-hand column keeping to their edges as the window resizes and the character to her seat, however quickly resizes that change the arrangement came before, and with reduced motion the arrangement changes at once. No screen is asked to be turned.

Turning to another section, or a body changing between one column and two, takes the title to the size the region now shown gives it, full for a column or a region at its top and tucked for one still scrolled down, in one glide rather than a jump, at once with reduced motion; a short card, as a phone held sideways, starts with its head tucked and its head rows drawn tighter. In one column, Save and share's picture for a friend with its settings, and its Keep working save options, each scroll together as that region, as Change planet's preview and planet list do.
