# Editing

This area covers how the editor changes a map: painting and erasing terrain and water, layers, corner cuts, undo and redo, selecting, moving, rotating and deleting objects, roads, bridges and ramps, curves, and how both views and the keyboard reach the same edits. The building rules every edit must pass are in the rules area.

## Painting terrain and water

- **EDIT-01** The terrain brush and the eraser draw in five shapes: freehand, line, curve, rectangle and circle. A line, rectangle or circle is laid only when the press is released, so it can be re-aimed while dragging, and holding the constrain key (Shift by default) squares a rectangle.
- **EDIT-02** A line, curve, rectangle or circle lays every part of itself the rules allow: a refused part, such as a stretch over the sea, a landmark or a locked layer, is skipped and the rest is laid.
- **EDIT-03** Outside the planet editor, a freehand mountain dab is a single mark: if any cell under the brush is refused, none of that dab is laid. A freehand water dab lays the cells that can hold water and skips the rest.
- **EDIT-04** A fast freehand drag paints every cell along the pointer's path, edge to edge, with no gaps between pointer samples.
- **EDIT-05** The mountain brush builds each cell up to the selected layer, filling the column beneath it.
- **EDIT-06** Painting over a mountain that already reaches the selected layer raises it by one layer, so repeated strokes over it keep climbing, while fresh ground beside it still gets the selected layer; the mountain brush never lowers terrain.
- **EDIT-07** A mountain asked to rise higher than its support allows settles at the highest layer the rules accept, and the editor names the support rule with its numbers. Source: V-MTN-03.
- **EDIT-08** Unless a layer is pinned, water lands on the surface under the brush, so one stroke down a terraced slope lays water on each terrace at that terrace's own level; with a layer pinned, water paints at that layer wherever the ground can hold it there.
- **EDIT-09** When the rules withdraw a water stroke and exactly one layer would hold that water, the editor names that layer.
- **EDIT-10** Painting mountain over water turns the water into mountain at the water's own level instead of stacking a block above it; a further stroke then stacks as usual.

## Erasing

- **EDIT-11** Each eraser takes only its own surface: the mountain eraser lowers a mountain by one layer per pass and skips water, the water eraser takes water and skips mountain, and the road eraser lifts road tiles and leaves the terrain beneath. A cell crossed more than once in one stroke is lowered once, and the whole gesture is one undo step.
- **EDIT-12** The water eraser turns raised water into mountain at the water's own level, and turns water at ground level back into grass.

## Layers

- **EDIT-13** The ground is the bottom layer and always exists, terrain stacks on it up to the game's highest layer, and every layer number the editor shows follows the game's numbering, in which the ground is layer 1.
- **EDIT-14** A locked layer refuses every edit that would change it, from any tool or shape, and automatic trimming leaves terrain standing on a locked layer as it is. Source: V-LOCK-01.
- **EDIT-15** The eraser and automatic trimming pass over terrain whose top stands on a hidden layer, leaving it as it is rather than refusing the stroke.

## Corner cuts

- **EDIT-16** The corner tool cycles each free corner of a terrain block from square to a quarter-circle round, then a 45-degree bevel, then back to square, and every corner at a junction cycles independently of the others. A corner joined along an edge to terrain of the same kind at the same height cannot be cut. A mountain capping a waterfall keeps its corner at the fall's lip square: the corner tool does not cut it, and any other edit that cuts it is accepted with that corner left square. Its corners away from the water cut like any other. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-22.
- **EDIT-17** Clicking the inner corner of an L-shaped notch rounds it with a fillet that rises as high as the surrounding walls wrap the corner, and cycling the fillet away returns the cell to what it held before.
- **EDIT-18** Corner cuts and fillets change only the outline drawn: they add and remove no support, so a block's support and an object's footing read the same with or without them.
- **EDIT-19** With Auto Trim set to Bevel or Round, a finished stroke cuts the corners it left exposed and fills the notches it formed, inside the stroke's own undo step; it never changes a corner whose surroundings the stroke left alone, and every automatic cut can be changed afterwards with the corner tool.
- **EDIT-20** When an edit changes the surroundings of a cut corner so that the cut no longer fits, the editor squares or removes it as part of the same edit, and undoing the edit restores the corner as it was.

## Undo and redo

- **EDIT-21** Each gesture is one undo step: a stroke, a shape, a corner click, a placement, a move, a rotation or a delete, together with the repairs it set off, such as roads carried to a new level, corners squared and automatic trims; each change of the object selection is one too (EDIT-71).
- **EDIT-22** Redo brings back exactly what undo took back, one step at a time, selection steps included, and a new edit or a new selection after an undo clears what could be redone. The part of a stroke the rules withdrew when it ended never reaches redo, and the steps before it stay undoable.
- **EDIT-75** Every edit the editor accepts, from any tool, gesture, undo or redo, leaves a map that every building rule accepts. Source: the rules area's "Every edit passes the same rules".
- **EDIT-23** A press cancelled before it ends, by a second touch or a lost pointer, takes back what it had laid, leaves nothing to redo and reports no refusal; the strokes before it stay. A finger's press reports what the rules refused once it travels past the drag threshold, is held for the hold time or lifts, so the first finger of a two-finger pan says nothing; a mouse press reports as it lands.
- **EDIT-71** Selecting and deselecting objects are undo steps: a click that selects, a Ctrl+click that adds or removes, a Ctrl+drag band (one step for the whole band), Ctrl+A, Escape, Done, a click that clears the selection, narrowing it on the selection card and the touch multi-select bar's taps are each one step, consecutive ones included; Undo gives back the selection before the step and Redo the one after. A write that changes nothing is no step, and neither is a selection put down by arming another tool or opening another map; while a tool that holds no selection is armed (EDIT-25), Undo and Redo leave the tool armed and the selection empty, passing over selection steps. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-72** An edit's undo step carries the selection around it: undoing the edit gives back the selection that stood before it and redoing it the selection it left, so undoing a delete brings its objects back selected and undoing a paste or a Ctrl+drag copy gives back the selection before it. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-73** Undo and redo never select an object that is no longer on the map: such members are left out of the selection they give back, and a selection step that would give back what already stands is passed over. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-74** Selection steps change nothing on the map: they count as no edit for the autosave, the restore offer and the arrival notice, Show me's steps or the assistant's undo floor, and the step history a save file carries holds the map's edits only, so save and share formats are unchanged; the Undo and Redo buttons count them. Selecting notes in Annotations is no step, since Notes keep their own undo. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Selection

- **EDIT-24** A click on an object selects it alone, Ctrl+click adds or removes it, Ctrl+drag selects every object whose footprint the band touches, Ctrl+A selects every object on the map including locked ones, and Escape clears the selection.
- **EDIT-25** A selection holds objects, or a single cell of mountain or water, never open ground, and it stands only while the Hand tool or the object placer is active: arming any other tool clears it. A plain click or tap on open ground, where no object stands and the cell holds no mountain or water, selects nothing and clears the selection; it picks that cell of the object grid as the place a paste starts from (EDIT-54), marked with the outline of that one cell, which the pointer's grey box shows before the click; a press over mountain or water selects that cell, which shows its card, and also picks the cell of the object grid under the pointer, marked by the selected cell's own outline. The picked block is no selection: Escape, selecting objects, arming a tool or item, painting a region, opening another map and a paste, whose copy becomes the selection, each drop it. Picking is no undo step of its own: a pick that clears selected objects is that click's one step (EDIT-71), whose undo gives back the objects without the pick, and Undo and Redo never give back a picked block. Source: owner report 2026-10-30, owner ruling 2026-10-30.
- **EDIT-26** Holding Ctrl while a terrain brush, the eraser or the corner tool is active selects objects instead of editing and then switches to the Hand tool; a band that catches nothing leaves the tool active and changes nothing.
- **EDIT-47** The selection card stands next to the selection with its header clear of the frame's controls beside it, and covers the selection only where the map left clear of the frame is too small to hold its header anywhere else; where there is no room for its opened details they scroll, and they may pass over a label higher up the right-hand column. In touch mode a name too long for the room wraps onto a second line before it is shortened.

## Moving, rotating and deleting objects

- **EDIT-27** Dragging a selected object moves it, or the whole selection when several are selected, as one undo step, and each object takes the height of the ground where it lands. A group lands every member or none: a refused move leaves every object where it stood and reports the rule that refused it.
- **EDIT-28** Rotating a selection turns it a quarter turn as one rigid body about the centre of its bounding box, so four turns in either direction bring every member exactly home; a square member that cannot turn is carried round with its facing unchanged, and a road tile's corner cuts turn with it.
- **EDIT-29** A rotation is refused whole, with nothing turned, when a member is locked, would land where the rules refuse it, or cannot turn, as a bridge or ramp cannot after placement; a single object whose turned footprint is refused stays as it was.
- **EDIT-30** Deleting a selection removes every unlocked member in one undo step and keeps the locked ones, saying how many it kept; a selection of locked objects only is refused and nothing changes.
- **EDIT-31** Before placing, the rotate shortcut turns the item waiting to be placed, its preview shows at once whether the turned item fits, and the turn carries over to the next placement of the same item; while objects are selected, the shortcut turns the selection instead.
- **EDIT-50** A card carried from the object shelf and released on the map stays armed after the release, as a click on the card would leave it, whether or not the rules took the placement (unless the placement filled the item's limit, EDIT-52); a carry released anywhere else, abandoned with Escape or cut off by the browser leaves armed what was armed before, with its pending turn, except that in touch mode a carry released without ever reaching the map adds the card to the armed items or takes it out (EDIT-70). Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-51** Before placing, the pointer or finger holds the item by the centre of its footprint (for an item laid on whole cells, on an even side, the cell left of or above the middle; an item that may stand on half cells, such as the station, centres on the half point nearest the pointer), by mouse, by touch and when carried from the shelf, in both views: the preview, its refusal mark and the placement on click, tap or release all stand there. A bridge or ramp is aimed by the gap or cliff under the pointer instead. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-52** Placing an item the map may hold only so many of, whether by a click or a card carried from the object shelf, puts it down once that placement fills its limit, since there is nothing more of it to place; an item with room left, or with no limit, stays armed. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Copying and pasting objects

- **EDIT-53** Copy (Ctrl+C, Cmd+C on a Mac, for any selection, or Copy on the selection card and the multi-select bar while two or more objects are selected) takes the selected objects as one arrangement: each object's item, its place and facing relative to the others and its height relative to the others, road and other surface tiles included with their corner cuts; terrain, water and mountain are never copied, and an object whose shape belongs to its own map, as a landmark does, is left out. A single object offers no Copy on its card or its menu, since Pick arms its item for placing again. Copying changes nothing on the map and keeps the selection, and a toast says how many objects were copied and how many, if any, were left out. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-54** Paste (Ctrl+V) places one copy at once, as one undo step, with nothing to confirm, and never reads where the pointer is: with a block picked (EDIT-25), the top-left of its outline goes on that block, as the map menu's Paste puts it on the pressed cell (EDIT-56); else with a region painted, the top-left of its outline goes on the first cell of the region in reading order whose copy fits wholly inside it; else the copy goes on the open ground nearest the place it was copied from, searched outward from the original group's top-left over the whole map, so a cut pasted back returns to its place and each further Paste lays another copy beside the original and the copies before it. A copy from another map starts from the place it was copied from there, held inside this map, and a copy an older release wrote, which records no place, starts from the middle of the map. The placed copy becomes the selection, so it can be moved, turned, deleted or copied on at once. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-29; owner report 2026-10-30.
- **EDIT-55** (retired 2026-10-29) Paste no longer arms a copy to turn before it is placed; a pasted copy is selected and turns as a selection does (EDIT-28, EDIT-54).
- **EDIT-56** With something copied, the right-click menu on the map, and in touch mode the long-press menu on an object or on open map, offers Paste, which places one copy with the top-left of its outline on the pressed cell, the press being the choice of where, and the placed copy becomes the selection. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-57** A copy is placed whole or not at all. Where it does not fit on the pressed cell, the picked block or inside the painted region, it goes to the nearest place a few cells from the aimed top-left where every member fits on ground no object covers, preferring ground at that cell's height, and a toast says it was placed nearby; a Ctrl+V with no region looks for such ground over the whole map, preferring ground at the height under the original group's top-left, and says nothing of nearby, since nearest is what it asked for. With no such place nothing changes and a toast says there is no room. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29, owner report 2026-10-30.
- **EDIT-58** A copy keeps its members' heights relative to one another: each member stands on the ground under it, and the copy fits only where that ground keeps the height differences the originals had, so an arrangement copied from one layer lands wholly on whatever single layer is under it and never splits across a cliff, and a bridge or ramp fits only where it spans as the original did. The layer chosen in the layer panel plays no part, and every placed copy passes every map rule: a solid member over a road removes the road as placing it would (EDIT-34), and a road member replaces a tile of its kind beneath it. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-59** A member whose item the map may hold only so many of is left out of a paste or a copy dragged with Ctrl once the map has no room left for it, so a one-of-a-kind building already on the map is never doubled, and the toast says how many were left out and why; the rest of the copy is placed. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-60** Holding Ctrl and dragging from a selected object drags a copy of the selection and leaves the originals where they stand; the drop places the copy where its preview stood as one undo step, refused whole with the rule that refused it, as a move is (EDIT-27), and the copy becomes the selection, so the next Ctrl+drag continues the pattern. A Ctrl+click on an object still adds or removes it, and a Ctrl+drag from anywhere else still selects by band (EDIT-24). Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-61** Cut (Ctrl+X) copies the selection and then deletes it as deleting a selection does (EDIT-30), keeping locked members. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-62** A copy also goes to the system clipboard as text, so Paste in another tab or window of the editor, on any map, places it there, and an editor open in another tab of the same browser takes the copy as its own, so its menus offer Paste at once. Pasted text is untrusted: text that is not a copy from the editor is ignored, a copy from a newer release is refused with a toast, members naming items this release does not know are left out and counted in the toast, and pasting into a text field pastes text as usual. Objects cannot be dragged from one tab into another; a cut in one tab and a paste in the other moves them. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-63** Copy and paste are not offered in the planet editor or during a hand repair. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Placing many objects

- **EDIT-64** A round brush button stands at the start of the object shelf's search, the search on its right: on a wide screen the button and the search field are one group no wider than the field alone was before the button joined it, and on an upright screen the button stands left of the search button in the names row, and beside the armed card while the shelf is folded to it. It stands on every tab and search of the shelf, unavailable where nothing the shelf shows can be laid more than one at a time (EDIT-69), and it is pressable with nothing armed, the way to place it sets then applying to the next card picked. Pressing it puts the brush bar in the shelf's place, as the region bar stands in for Generate's shelf: the two ways to place, Place (key 1) and Paint (key 2), where the terrain bar keeps its tools, the brush shape (freehand, line, curve, rectangle, circle; key 5 steps through them) and the spacing, both for Paint only, and Done, which gives back the shelf on the tab it was left on with what was armed still armed and the way to place kept. On a wide screen in PC mode a mouse resting on the button, or the keyboard's focus, grows it along the row into a pill naming the way to place now chosen, the search field shrinking by as much so the group keeps its width, in one motion each way (at once with reduced motion); in touch mode and on an upright screen it stays round. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-65** Place places one item per click or tap, as an armed card always has. With Paint chosen, from the brush bar or straight from the shelf, a press lays items along the brush shape: a freehand drag along its path as it goes, a line along the dragged line, a curve along its path once a double-click finishes it (EDIT-36), and a rectangle or circle over its whole area, each previewed before it is laid. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-66** Spacing, from 0 to 4 cells, is the number of empty cells kept between neighbouring items of a Paint stroke; an item larger than one cell keeps its own footprint plus the spacing, so the items of a stroke never overlap, and a rectangle lays its rows and columns from the corner it was dragged from. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-67** Each item a Paint stroke lays is placed as a single placement is, through every map rule, standing on the ground under it and removing a road under a solid item as a placement does (EDIT-34); a spot the rules refuse is skipped and the rest of the stroke is laid, unlike a paste (EDIT-57), and a toast after the stroke says how many spots were skipped, or, where the rules refused every spot, names the rule that refused the first, as a refused placement does. The whole stroke is one undo step, and a stroke that laid nothing leaves none. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-68** The shelf's brush button wears the way to place now chosen: Place's drawing while Place is chosen, and while Paint is chosen the active colour and the drawing of the current brush shape, so the shelf shows that a press lays a spread rather than one item; while it is unavailable (EDIT-69) it wears Place's drawing, dimmed as every unavailable control is. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-69** An item the map may hold only so many of (EDIT-52), and a bridge or ramp, which aims at a gap or cliff (EDIT-35), is placed one at a time: with one armed the brush button is unavailable and says why when reached for, and with Paint chosen a press places one item as Place does; on a shelf tab or search showing only such items the brush button is unavailable too and says that the items there are placed one at a time. While the planting card is armed, which lays its own planting, the brush button is unavailable too and says why. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-70** Ctrl+click on a shelf card (Cmd+click on a Mac), or in touch mode a long press on a card that ends without reaching the map, adds its item to the armed items or takes it out again, so several cards are armed together in the order they were picked; Place then places them in turn, one per placement, and each Paint stroke lays them in turn along its shape from the first picked (abab). A plain click arms one card alone, and an item of EDIT-69 is never armed beside another: Ctrl+click on it arms it alone. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Locked objects

- **EDIT-32** Outside the planet editor, the landmarks that come with a planet are locked: they can be selected but never moved, turned or removed, and clearing every object leaves them in place. Source: V-LOCK-02.

## Roads, bridges and ramps

- **EDIT-33** Road tiles are laid one per cell on land, never on water, and a tile of a new material replaces the one beneath it. Painting mountain under a road carries the road to its new level when its tile stays level and removes it when the ground under it turns uneven, within the same undo step. Source: V-PLACE-BLOCK.
- **EDIT-34** Placing a tree or flower over an ordinary road with the object placer, or moving one there, removes the road beneath it in the same undo step, and a refused placement leaves the road untouched. Source: V-PLACE-OVERLAP.
- **EDIT-35** A bridge placed at a gap snaps across it, taking the position, orientation, length and deck height the gap allows, and a ramp placed at a cliff snaps up it in whichever direction the cliff faces; both may sit on half cells to lie flush with the terrain, and their orientation always comes from the gap or cliff, never from the rotate command. A bridge or ramp dragged to a new place snaps again where it is dropped. Source: V-PLACE-TRAIT.

## Curves

- **EDIT-36** A curve paints nothing until a double-click finishes it; Delete takes back the last anchor, and Escape abandons an unfinished curve without painting anything.
- **EDIT-37** Once a curve is drawn, dragging an anchor or a direction handle lays the whole curve again along the new path: the old path returns to what it held before the curve, mountain never stacks twice where the paths overlap, and each adjustment is its own undo step. Escape, a click elsewhere or a change of shape or surface puts the handles away and keeps the curve.
- **EDIT-38** An adjustment the rules refuse puts both the map and the curve's handles back as they were and leaves no undo step behind.

## Views and shortcuts

- **EDIT-39** The 2D and 3D views run the same tools and the same edits: a gesture in either view makes the same change, refusal and undo step, and a curve being adjusted carries over when the view switches.
- **EDIT-40** A keyboard shortcut runs the same action as its button or tile, with the same result and the same refusal message, and shortcuts do nothing while the user types in a text field.

## Touch

- **EDIT-41** In touch mode an armed item shows under the finger while it is held and is placed on release; a release over the object bar places nothing, and a release on a refused cell places nothing. A card carried from the object shelf folds the shelf down to the bar, which shows only that a release over it cancels.
- **EDIT-42** In touch mode a long press on an object opens its menu, headed by the object's name in full (rotate and pick where the selection card offers them, move where the object can move, multi-select, paste once something is copied, delete); with something copied, a long press on open map opens a menu offering paste. Its rotate turns the object a quarter clockwise as the selection card's does, and the release that ends the long press chooses no row. A long press with a tool armed does what holding still does for that tool. A menu keeps the rows of the press that opened it whatever the mode becomes, so the mouse's right-click menu keeps its own rows.
- **EDIT-43** Multi-select in touch mode latches: taps add or remove objects, a drag from empty map selects by band, a drag from a selected object moves the group, and Done ends it and gives back the bar on the shelf tab it was left on.
- **EDIT-44** In touch mode a two-finger tap undoes one step.
- **EDIT-45** In touch mode with nothing armed, a one-finger drag pans unless it starts on the selection, which it moves, and a tap selects.
- **EDIT-46** While a placement or a brush, eraser, edge cut or note tool is pressed in touch mode, a magnifier shows the cells under the finger.
- **EDIT-48** In touch mode with nothing armed and Multi-select off, a double tap on the map where no object stands zooms in one step around the finger and leaves the selection as it was; otherwise a double tap does what a mouse double click does there, so with the curve brush it finishes the curve. Source: owner ruling, 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-49** A finger or pen on an object shelf card that can be carried, whichever input mode is in force, scrolls the shelf when it moves and lifts the card into the hand once held still for the hold time; while it is held, a ring above the finger, inside the window, fills over exactly that time and goes the moment the press scrolls, lifts the card or ends, and no other press removes it. A mouse press on a card shows no ring. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
