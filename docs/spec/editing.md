# Editing

This area covers how the editor changes a map: painting and erasing terrain and water, layers, corner cuts, undo and redo, selecting, moving, rotating and deleting objects, roads, bridges and ramps, curves, and how both views and the keyboard reach the same edits. The building rules every edit must pass are in the rules area.

## Painting terrain and water

- **EDIT-01** The terrain brush and the eraser draw in five shapes: freehand, line, curve, rectangle and circle. A line, rectangle or circle is laid only when the press is released, so it can be re-aimed while dragging, and holding the constrain key (Shift by default) squares a rectangle.
- **EDIT-02** A line, curve, rectangle or circle lays every part of itself the rules allow: a refused part, such as a stretch over the sea, a landmark or a locked layer, is skipped and the rest is laid. Each shape works on the one layer its press starts on, as a freehand stroke does (EDIT-05, EDIT-08, EDIT-11): a line or rectangle where its drag starts, a circle at its centre and a curve at its first anchor, which its adjustments keep. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-03** Outside the planet editor, a freehand mountain dab is a single mark: if any cell under the brush is refused, none of that dab is laid. A freehand water dab lays the cells that can hold water and skips the rest.
- **EDIT-04** A fast freehand drag paints every cell along the pointer's path, edge to edge, with no gaps between pointer samples.
- **EDIT-05** A mountain stroke starts on the surface S of the cell under its press (one layer below a water's own level, so water there turns into mountain at that level, EDIT-10) and builds the brush's thickness T, 1 to 3 layers (EDIT-80): across the whole stroke, every cell whose surface stands from S up to below S + T rises to S + T, never past the game's highest layer, and a cell lower than S is never built up to the stroke. With a thickness of 1 the stroke adds the one layer above where it starts. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-06** Cells already at the stroke's top or higher stay as they are, however often the stroke crosses them, so a stroke never stacks; a new press on a cell builds on that cell's own top, and the mountain brush never lowers terrain. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-07** A stroke that would raise mountain beyond the support the rules ask for settles at the highest layer the rules accept when it ends, leaving cells lower where the support runs out, and the editor names the support rule with its numbers. Source: V-MTN-03; owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-08** The water brush works on one layer per stroke, the surface height of the cell the press starts on: it lays water at that height only on ground or blocks whose top stands at that height, turning such a block into water at its own height, and leaves every other cell as it is, so water never lowers a mountain or lands on another layer. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-09** (retired 2026-11-07) Water lands only at the height its stroke starts on (EDIT-08), and a refused water stroke names the rule that refused it.
- **EDIT-10** Painting mountain over water turns the water into mountain at the water's own level instead of stacking a block above it; a further stroke then stacks as usual.

## Erasing

- **EDIT-11** Each eraser takes only its own surface, and the mountain and water erasers only from the top S of the cell their press starts on: the mountain eraser lowers every mountain whose top stands above S − T and no higher than S to S − T, T being the brush's thickness (EDIT-80), never below the ground, and skips water; the water eraser takes the water at S and skips mountain; every cell higher than S, or at S − T and lower, stays as it is. The road eraser lifts road tiles and leaves the terrain beneath. A cell crossed more than once in one stroke is lowered once, and the whole gesture is one undo step. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-12** The water eraser turns raised water into mountain at the water's own level, and turns water at ground level back into grass.

## Layers

- **EDIT-13** The ground is the bottom layer and always exists, terrain stacks on it up to the game's highest layer, and every layer number the editor shows follows the game's numbering, in which the ground is layer 1.
- **EDIT-14** A locked layer refuses every edit that would change it, from any tool or shape, and automatic trimming leaves terrain standing on a locked layer as it is. Source: V-LOCK-01.
- **EDIT-15** The eraser and automatic trimming pass over terrain whose top stands on a hidden layer, leaving it as it is rather than refusing the stroke.
- **EDIT-79** No control chooses a layer by hand, and the layer panel plays no part in where terrain is built: it keeps showing and hiding and locking layers, and it and the layer control show the layer each terrain stroke works on, the top a mountain stroke builds, the height water lies at or the top an eraser starts from, from the press to the stroke's end and after it until the tool or mode changes. The layer control shows that layer's name beside an arrow plate, as the game draws it; pressing the name or the plate opens the layer panel, and neither steps a layer. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner rulings 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-80** The mountain brush, its shapes and the mountain eraser offer Thickness, a group of three options between Auto Trim and the size slider, each drawing three stacked slabs with as many solid from the bottom as the layers it stands for and the rest faint, and no number; it sets how many layers a stroke builds or takes, 1 by default, and its key (L by default) steps through 1, 2 and 3. Choosing a thickness is a setting, never an undo step. Water, roads, the other tools and the planet editor show no such group. Source: player report 2026-11-05, owner ruling 2026-11-07.

## Corner cuts

- **EDIT-16** The corner tool cycles each free corner of a terrain block from square to a quarter-circle round, then a 45-degree bevel, then back to square, and every corner at a junction cycles independently of the others. A corner joined along an edge to terrain of the same kind at the same height cannot be cut. A mountain capping a waterfall keeps its corner at the fall's lip square: the corner tool does not cut it, and any other edit that cuts it is accepted with that corner left square. Its corners away from the water cut like any other. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-22.
- **EDIT-17** Clicking the inner corner of an L-shaped notch rounds it with a fillet that rises as high as the surrounding walls wrap the corner, and cycling the fillet away returns the cell to what it held before.
- **EDIT-18** Corner cuts and fillets change only the outline drawn: they add and remove no support, so a block's support and an object's footing read the same with or without them.
- **EDIT-19** With Auto Trim set to Bevel or Round, a finished stroke cuts the corners it left exposed and fills the notches it formed, inside the stroke's own undo step; it never changes a corner whose surroundings the stroke left alone, and every automatic cut can be changed afterwards with the corner tool.
- **EDIT-20** When an edit changes the surroundings of a cut corner so that the cut no longer fits, the editor squares or removes it as part of the same edit, and undoing the edit restores the corner as it was.

## Undo and redo

- **EDIT-21** Each gesture is one undo step: a stroke, a shape, a corner click, a placement, a move, a rotation or a delete, together with the repairs it set off, such as roads carried to a new level, corners squared and automatic trims; each change of the object selection is one too (EDIT-71).
- **EDIT-22** Redo brings back exactly what undo took back, one step at a time, selection steps included, and a new edit or a new selection after an undo clears what could be redone. The part of a stroke the rules withdrew when it ended never reaches redo, and the steps before it stay undoable.
- **EDIT-75** Every edit the editor accepts, from any tool, gesture, undo or redo, leaves a map that every building rule accepts. Source: the rules area's "Every edit passes the same rules".
- **EDIT-23** A press cancelled before it ends, by a second touch or a lost pointer, takes back what it had laid, leaves nothing to redo and reports no refusal; the strokes before it stay. A finger's press reports what the rules refused once it travels past the drag threshold, is held for the hold time or lifts, so the first finger of a two-finger pan says nothing; a mouse press reports as it lands.
- **EDIT-71** Selecting and deselecting objects are undo steps: a click that selects, a Ctrl+click that adds or removes, a Ctrl+drag band (one step for the whole band), Ctrl+A, Escape, Done, a click that clears the selection, narrowing it on the selection card and the touch multi-select bar's taps are each one step, consecutive ones included; Undo gives back the selection before the step and Redo the one after. A write that changes nothing is no step, and neither is a selection put down by arming another tool, opening another map or hiding the layer it stands on; while a tool that holds no selection is armed (EDIT-25), Undo and Redo leave the tool armed and the selection empty, passing over selection steps. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-72** An edit's undo step carries the selection around it: undoing the edit gives back the selection that stood before it and redoing it the selection it left, so undoing a delete brings its objects back selected and undoing a paste or a Ctrl+drag copy gives back the selection before it. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-73** Undo and redo never select an object that is no longer on the map: such members are left out of the selection they give back, and a selection step that would give back what already stands is passed over. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-74** Selection steps change nothing on the map: they count as no edit for the autosave, the restore offer and the arrival notice, Show me's steps or the assistant's undo floor, and the step history a save file carries holds the map's edits only, so save and share formats are unchanged; the Undo and Redo buttons count them. Selecting notes in Annotations is no step, since Notes keep their own undo. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Selection

- **EDIT-24** A click on an object selects it alone, Ctrl+click adds or removes it, Ctrl+drag selects every object whose footprint the band touches, Ctrl+A selects every object on the map including locked ones, and Escape clears the selection; with an item armed and an object selected under it, the first Escape clears the selection and the next puts the item down, as Escape always ends the newest state that still stands (UI-12). Source: owner ruling 2026-11-06. A Ctrl+click, Ctrl+drag or Ctrl+A that selects something with an item or a blueprint armed puts it down, since the selection is now what the keys act on.
- **EDIT-25** A selection holds objects, or a single cell of mountain or water, never open ground, and it stands only while the Hand tool or the object placer is active, or in Blueprint mode. It ends when the user moves on to something else: arming any other tool, arming something to place (picking an item or blueprint card, adding a card to the armed ones, carrying a card or blueprint, even the one already armed, or coming back to Objects or Blueprint mode with a card or blueprint still armed), or starting a region or frame step (Generate's or the assistant's region, Blueprint's New blueprint or Save as blueprint), or unfolding Blueprint's explorer (BLUE-03), or leaving Blueprint mode with a placement selected whole (BLUE-07), so the rotate and flip shortcuts, Delete and the selection card answer for what comes next, and the delete question and a menu opened on the selection close with it. Backing out of what took its place gives the selection back: a carry that never lands (EDIT-50), a frame step left with Cancel or Escape (BLUE-04) and Blueprint's explorer folding (BLUE-03). Putting something down is no move: a selection made while an item is armed, by a click where it cannot be placed, stands when the item is put down, and a placed blueprint stays selected (BLUE-06). A plain click or tap on open ground, where no object stands and the cell holds no mountain or water, selects nothing and clears the selection; it picks that cell of the object grid as the place a paste starts from (EDIT-54), marked with the outline of that one cell, which the pointer's grey box shows before the click; a press over mountain or water selects that cell, which shows its card, and also picks the cell of the object grid under the pointer, marked by the selected cell's own outline. The picked block is no selection: Escape, selecting objects, arming a tool or item, painting a region, opening another map and a paste, whose copy becomes the selection, each drop it. Opening another map ends the selection, the picked block and the touch latches, and closes the delete question and the map's menu. Picking is no undo step of its own: a pick that clears selected objects is that click's one step (EDIT-71), whose undo gives back the objects without the pick, and Undo and Redo never give back a picked block. Source: owner report 2026-10-30, owner ruling 2026-10-30; owner report 2026-11-06.
- **EDIT-76** Notes selected in Notes stay selected only while Notes is open: leaving Notes for another mode or none ends the selection, so a colour or tag chosen afterwards reaches no note.
- **EDIT-77** Nothing on a hidden layer can be selected or acted on: a press, a Ctrl band and Select all pass over the objects standing on a hidden build floor and the mountain or water cells whose top is on it, as they pass over the notes of a hidden Notes layer. Hiding a layer ends the selection of what stands on it: hiding a build floor drops the selected objects standing on it and a selected mountain or water cell whose top is on it, closing the delete question or menu about one of them, and hiding the Notes layer drops the selected notes; the rest of the selection stands, so Delete, the rotate and flip keys and a colour chosen afterwards never reach what is hidden.
- **EDIT-78** The map's menu and the delete question stand only over what they were opened on: an edit, Undo or Redo of the map by anyone, another map, another mode, taking up something to place or a region or frame step, carrying a card or blueprint to the map, unfolding Blueprint's explorer, their object or cell no longer being there or standing on a hidden floor, or the view moving or zooming under them closes them, so no row acts on a target that changed and Escape and Delete answer as with neither open; the touch menu's own rotate leaves it open for the next turn (EDIT-42), in the 3D view as in 2D. A right-click opens the menu after the selection the press makes, so a Ctrl right-click that puts an armed card down keeps its menu.
- **EDIT-26** Holding Ctrl while a terrain brush, the eraser or the corner tool is active selects objects instead of editing and then switches to the Hand tool; a band that catches nothing leaves the tool active and changes nothing.
- **EDIT-47** The selection card stands next to the selection with its header clear of the frame's controls beside it, and covers the selection only where the map left clear of the frame is too small to hold its header anywhere else; where there is no room for its opened details they scroll inside the card's rounded corners, fading at an edge with more beyond it, and a mouse wheel over them scrolls them alone, never the map, at either end too; they may pass over a label higher up the right-hand column. In touch mode a name too long for the room wraps onto a second line before it is shortened. Where the room holds the card's controls but not the shortest name, the card leaves the name out, and for several objects the count on its thumbnail stands for it. A selection holding an object offers Save as blueprint on the card wherever Blueprint mode can open, which opens that mode's frame step drawn around the selection (BLUE-04).
- **EDIT-81** In Notes, the row over the selected notes offers Merge when two or more of them are zones, which joins those zones into the first selected one and leaves it selected, Flip when one or more of them is a measurement, which draws each on its other side, and Delete, which takes every one of them; Merge and Flip leave a selected note of another kind as it is. On a locked layer the row shows its verbs unavailable.

## Moving, rotating and deleting objects

- **EDIT-27** Dragging a selected object moves it, or the whole selection when several are selected, as one undo step, and each object takes the height of the ground where it lands. A group lands every member or none: a refused move leaves every object where it stood and reports the rule that refused it.
- **EDIT-28** Rotating a selection turns it a quarter turn as one rigid body about the centre of its bounding box, so four turns in either direction bring every member exactly home; a square member that cannot turn is carried round with its facing unchanged, and a road tile's corner cuts turn with it.
- **EDIT-29** A rotation is refused whole, with nothing turned, when a member is locked, would land where the rules refuse it, or cannot turn, as a bridge or ramp cannot after placement; a single object whose turned footprint is refused stays as it was.
- **EDIT-30** Deleting a selection removes every unlocked member in one undo step and keeps the locked ones, saying how many it kept; a selection of locked objects only, a single one included, is refused at once with the message that it is locked, by the key or the map's menu alike, with no question asked, and nothing changes.
- **EDIT-31** Before placing, the rotate shortcut turns the item waiting to be placed, its preview shows at once whether the turned item fits, and the turn carries over to the next placement of the same item; arming the item clears any selection made before it (EDIT-25), and while objects selected with the item armed stand selected, the shortcut turns them instead.
- **EDIT-50** A card carried from the object shelf and released on the map stays armed after the release, as a click on the card would leave it, whether or not the rules took the placement (unless the placement filled the item's limit, EDIT-52); the carry clears the selection, even for the card already armed (EDIT-25); a carry released anywhere else, abandoned with Escape or cut off by the browser leaves armed what was armed before, with its pending turn, and gives back the selection it cleared, except that in touch mode a carry released without ever reaching the map adds the card to the armed items or takes it out (EDIT-70); a change of mode during the carry ends it at once, so a later release places nothing, and the new mode keeps what it armed, with no selection given back, as a carried blueprint does. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-51** Before placing, the pointer or finger holds the item by the centre of its footprint (for an item laid on whole cells, on an even side, the cell left of or above the middle; an item that may stand on half cells, such as the station, centres on the half point nearest the pointer), by mouse, by touch and when carried from the shelf, in both views: the preview, its refusal mark and the placement on click, tap or release all stand there. A bridge or ramp is aimed by the gap or cliff under the pointer instead. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-52** Placing an item the map may hold only so many of, whether by a click or a card carried from the object shelf, puts it down once that placement fills its limit, since there is nothing more of it to place; an item with room left, or with no limit, stays armed. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Copying and pasting objects

- **EDIT-53** Copy (Ctrl+C, Cmd+C on a Mac, for any selection, or Copy on the selection card and the multi-select bar while two or more objects are selected) takes the selected objects as one arrangement: each object's item, its place and facing relative to the others and its height relative to the others, road and other surface tiles included with their corner cuts; terrain, water and mountain are never copied, and an object whose shape belongs to its own map, as a landmark does, is left out. A placed blueprint selected whole in Blueprint mode is copied whole with its terrain instead (BLUE-07), its selection card offering Copy, and the toast says the blueprint was copied. A single object offers no Copy on its card or its menu, since Pick arms its item for placing again. Copying changes nothing on the map and keeps the selection, and a toast says how many objects were copied and how many, if any, were left out. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29; owner pick 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-54** Paste (Ctrl+V) places one copy at once, as one undo step, with nothing to confirm, and never reads where the pointer is: with a block picked (EDIT-25), the top-left of its outline goes on that block, as the map menu's Paste puts it on the pressed cell (EDIT-56); else with a region on the map (the one being painted, else Generate's scope while Generate is open, else the assistant's while its panel is open; never a blueprint's frame once its step ends), the top-left of its outline goes on the first cell of the region in reading order whose copy fits wholly inside it; else the copy goes on the open ground nearest the place it was copied from, searched outward from the original group's top-left over the whole map, so a cut pasted back returns to its place and each further Paste lays another copy beside the original and the copies before it. A copy from another map starts from the place it was copied from there, held inside this map, and a copy an older release wrote, which records no place, starts from the middle of the map. A copy of a placed blueprint (BLUE-07) aims the same way with its frame as its outline. The placed copy becomes the selection, so it can be moved, turned, deleted or copied on at once; a placed blueprint's copy is selected whole in Blueprint mode and by its objects elsewhere. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-29; owner report 2026-10-30; owner pick 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-55** (retired 2026-10-29) Paste no longer arms a copy to turn before it is placed; a pasted copy is selected and turns as a selection does (EDIT-28, EDIT-54).
- **EDIT-56** With something copied, the right-click menu on the map, and in touch mode the long-press menu on an object or on open map, offers Paste, which places one copy with the top-left of its outline on the pressed cell, the press being the choice of where, and the placed copy becomes the selection. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-57** A copy is placed whole or not at all. Where it does not fit on the pressed cell, the picked block or inside the painted region, it goes to the nearest place a few cells from the aimed top-left where every member fits on ground no object covers, preferring ground at that cell's height, and a toast says it was placed nearby; a Ctrl+V with no region looks for such ground over the whole map, preferring ground at the height under the original group's top-left, and says nothing of nearby, since nearest is what it asked for. Ground at the preferred height wins only while it lies at most a few cells farther than the nearest such ground at another height. With no such place nothing changes and a toast says there is no room. A copy of a placed blueprint follows the blueprint's own rule instead (BLUE-06, BLUE-08): on the picked block or the pressed cell it lands wherever anything of it lands there, skipping what cannot and saying so; in a painted region and with no block picked it takes only a place where all of it lands on open ground, nothing skipped and its frame overlapping no placement's; and where nothing of it lands on the cell, or no such place stands in the region, it goes to the nearest such place as above, preferring the layer the aimed top-left gives it, or with no block picked the layer it was copied from. Source: owner request 2026-10-29, owner ruling 2026-10-29, owner report 2026-10-30; owner pick 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-58** A copy keeps its members' heights relative to one another: each member stands on the ground under it, and the copy fits only where that ground keeps the height differences the originals had, so an arrangement copied from one layer lands wholly on whatever single layer is under it and never splits across a cliff, and a bridge or ramp fits only where it spans as the original did. The layer the layer panel shows plays no part, and every placed copy passes every map rule: a solid member over a road removes the road as placing it would (EDIT-34), and a road member replaces a tile of its kind beneath it. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-59** A member whose item the map may hold only so many of is left out of a paste or a copy dragged with Ctrl once the map has no room left for it, so a one-of-a-kind building already on the map is never doubled, and the toast says how many were left out and why; the rest of the copy is placed. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-60** Holding Ctrl and dragging from a selected object drags a copy of the selection and leaves the originals where they stand; the drop places the copy where its preview stood as one undo step, refused whole with the rule that refused it, as a move is (EDIT-27), and the copy becomes the selection, so the next Ctrl+drag continues the pattern. In Blueprint mode, with a placed blueprint selected whole, a Ctrl+drag from any of its objects or any cell of its frame drags a copy of it whole with its terrain, shown as the blueprint's ghost (UI-69), and leaves it where it stands; the drop lays the copy as one undo step under the blueprint's rule (BLUE-06, BLUE-08), skipping and saying what cannot land and laying nothing where nothing of it would, and the copy is selected whole. A Ctrl+click on an object still adds or removes it, and a Ctrl+drag from anywhere else still selects by band (EDIT-24). Source: owner request 2026-10-29; owner pick 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-61** Cut (Ctrl+X) copies the selection and then deletes it as deleting a selection does (EDIT-30), keeping locked members; a placed blueprint selected whole is copied whole and taken back as its Delete takes it (BLUE-07). Source: owner request 2026-10-29; owner pick 2026-11-07.
- **EDIT-62** A copy also goes to the system clipboard as text, so Paste in another tab or window of the editor, on any map, places it there, and an editor open in another tab of the same browser takes the copy as its own, so its menus offer Paste at once. Pasted text is untrusted: text that is not a copy from the editor is ignored, a copy from a newer release is refused with a toast, members naming items this release does not know are left out and counted in the toast, and pasting into a text field pastes text as usual. A placed blueprint's copy carries its terrain, its blueprint and its name and original author, which the receiving editor shows only once the background text review approves them (BLUE-07); an older release still pastes a copy of objects and refuses a copy of a placed blueprint as one from a newer release. Objects cannot be dragged from one tab into another; a cut in one tab and a paste in the other moves them. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-63** Copy and paste are not offered in the planet editor or during a hand repair. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Placing many objects

- **EDIT-64** A round brush button stands at the start of the object shelf's search, the search on its right: on a wide screen the button and the search field are one group no wider than the field alone was before the button joined it, and on an upright screen the button stands left of the search button in the names row, and beside the armed card while the shelf is folded to it. It stands on every tab and search of the shelf, unavailable where nothing the shelf shows can be laid more than one at a time (EDIT-69), and it is pressable with nothing armed, the way to place it sets then applying to the next card picked. Pressing it puts the brush bar in the shelf's place, as the region bar stands in for Generate's shelf: the two ways to place, Place (key 1) and Paint (key 2), where the terrain bar keeps its tools, the brush shape (freehand, line, curve, rectangle, circle; key 5 steps through them) and the spacing, both for Paint only, and Done, which gives back the shelf on the tab it was left on with what was armed still armed and the way to place kept. A card armed while the bar stands is part of it: one Escape puts the bar away with that card, leaving nothing armed. On a wide screen in PC mode a mouse resting on the button, or the keyboard's focus, grows it along the row into a pill naming the way to place now chosen, the search field shrinking by as much so the group keeps its width, in one motion each way (at once with reduced motion); in touch mode and on an upright screen it stays round. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-65** Place places one item per click or tap, as an armed card always has. With Paint chosen, from the brush bar or straight from the shelf, a press lays items along the brush shape: a freehand drag along its path as it goes, a line along the dragged line, a curve along its path once a double-click finishes it (EDIT-36), and a rectangle or circle over its whole area, each previewed before it is laid. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-66** Spacing, from 0 to 4 cells, is the number of empty cells kept between neighbouring items of a Paint stroke; an item larger than one cell keeps its own footprint plus the spacing, so the items of a stroke never overlap, and a rectangle lays its rows and columns from the corner it was dragged from. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-67** Each item a Paint stroke lays is placed as a single placement is, through every map rule, standing on the ground under it and removing a road under a solid item as a placement does (EDIT-34); a spot the rules refuse is skipped and the rest of the stroke is laid, unlike a paste (EDIT-57), and a toast after the stroke says how many spots were skipped, or, where the rules refused every spot, names the rule that refused the first, as a refused placement does. The whole stroke is one undo step, and a stroke that laid nothing leaves none. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-68** The shelf's brush button wears the way to place now chosen: Place's drawing while Place is chosen, and while Paint is chosen the active colour and the drawing of the current brush shape, so the shelf shows that a press lays a spread rather than one item; while it is unavailable (EDIT-69) it wears Place's drawing, dimmed as every unavailable control is. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-69** An item the map may hold only so many of (EDIT-52), and a bridge or ramp, which aims at a gap or cliff (EDIT-35), is placed one at a time: with one armed the brush button is unavailable and says why when reached for, and with Paint chosen a press places one item as Place does; on a shelf tab or search showing only such items the brush button is unavailable too and says that the items there are placed one at a time. While the planting card is armed, which lays its own planting, the brush button is unavailable too and says why. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.
- **EDIT-70** Ctrl+click on a shelf card (Cmd+click on a Mac), or in touch mode a long press on a card that ends without reaching the map, adds its item to the armed items or takes it out again, so several cards are armed together in the order they were picked; Place then places them in turn, one per placement, and each Paint stroke lays them in turn along its shape from the first picked (abab). A plain click arms one card alone, and an item of EDIT-69 is never armed beside another: Ctrl+click on it arms it alone. Source: owner request 2026-10-29.

## Locked objects

- **EDIT-32** Outside the planet editor, the landmarks that come with a planet are locked: they can be selected but never moved, turned or removed, and clearing every object leaves them in place. Source: V-LOCK-02.

## Roads, bridges and ramps

- **EDIT-33** Road tiles are laid one per cell on land, never on water, and a tile of a new material replaces the one beneath it. Painting mountain under a road carries the road to its new level when its tile stays level and removes it when the ground under it turns uneven, within the same undo step. Source: V-PLACE-BLOCK.
- **EDIT-34** Placing a tree or flower over an ordinary road with the object placer, or moving one there, removes the road beneath it in the same undo step, and a refused placement leaves the road untouched. Source: V-PLACE-OVERLAP.
- **EDIT-35** A bridge placed at a gap snaps across it, taking the position, orientation, length and deck height the gap allows, and a ramp placed at a cliff snaps up it in whichever direction the cliff faces; both may sit on half cells to lie flush with the terrain, and their orientation always comes from the gap or cliff, never from the rotate command. A bridge or ramp dragged to a new place snaps again where it is dropped. Source: V-PLACE-TRAIT.
- **EDIT-82** The road bar's switch between road surfaces and surface prints arms the material last laid in the family it switches to, or that family's first until one was laid there, and keeps both for as long as the page is open, across other modes and other maps.

## Curves

- **EDIT-36** A curve paints nothing until a double-click finishes it; Delete takes back the last anchor, and Escape abandons an unfinished curve without painting anything.
- **EDIT-37** Once a curve is drawn, dragging an anchor or a direction handle lays the whole curve again along the new path: the old path returns to what it held before the curve, mountain never stacks twice where the paths overlap, and each adjustment is its own undo step. Escape, a click elsewhere or a change of shape or surface puts the handles away and keeps the curve.
- **EDIT-38** An adjustment the rules refuse puts both the map and the curve's handles back as they were and leaves no undo step behind.

## Views and shortcuts

- **EDIT-39** The 2D and 3D views run the same tools and the same edits: a gesture in either view makes the same change, refusal and undo step, and a curve being adjusted carries over when the view switches.
- **EDIT-40** A keyboard shortcut runs the same action as its button or tile, with the same result and the same refusal message, and shortcuts do nothing while the user types in a text field, except that What's this? and the interface scale keys answer there to a press that types nothing: an F key, or a Ctrl or Cmd chord.

## Touch

- **EDIT-41** In touch mode an armed item shows under the finger while it is held and is placed on release; a release over the object bar places nothing, and a release on a refused cell places nothing. A card carried from the object shelf folds the shelf down to the bar, which shows only that a release over it cancels.
- **EDIT-42** In touch mode a long press on an object opens its menu, headed by the object's name in full (rotate and pick where the selection card offers them, move where the object can move, multi-select, paste once something is copied, delete); with something copied, a long press on open map opens a menu offering paste. Its rotate turns the object a quarter clockwise as the selection card's does, and the release that ends the long press chooses no row. A long press with a tool armed does what holding still does for that tool. A menu keeps the rows of the press that opened it whatever the mode becomes, so the mouse's right-click menu keeps its own rows.
- **EDIT-43** Multi-select in touch mode latches: taps add or remove objects, a drag from empty map selects by band, a drag from a selected object moves the group, and Done ends it and gives back the bar on the shelf tab it was left on. Its bar offers Rotate, Flip, Copy and Delete where the selection card would offer them for the same selection, and Move while anything is selected. The bar also shows how many objects are selected and Done, and nothing of multi-select stands over the map: upright, the count and Done stand on a line above the actions, which scroll sideways where they do not fit; on a wide window the count leads the row of actions and Done ends it.
- **EDIT-44** In touch mode a two-finger tap undoes one step.
- **EDIT-45** In touch mode with nothing armed, a one-finger drag pans unless it starts on the selection, which it moves, and a tap selects.
- **EDIT-46** While a placement or a brush, eraser, edge cut or note tool is pressed in touch mode, a magnifier shows the cells under the finger.
- **EDIT-48** In touch mode with nothing armed and Multi-select off, a double tap on the map where no object stands zooms in one step around the finger and leaves the selection as it was; otherwise a double tap does what a mouse double click does there, so with the curve brush it finishes the curve. Source: owner ruling, 2026-10-24.
- **EDIT-49** A finger or pen on an object shelf card that can be carried, whichever input mode is in force, scrolls the shelf when it moves and lifts the card into the hand once held still for the hold time; while it is held, a ring above the finger, inside the window, fills over exactly that time and goes the moment the press scrolls, lifts the card or ends, and no other press removes it. A mouse press on a card shows no ring. Source: owner ruling 2026-10-24.
