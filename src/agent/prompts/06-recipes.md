# RECIPES (proven legal patterns — adapt sizes freely)
- Ground lake: water needs no caps at elevation 0 when surrounded by flat ground at the same level — paint_terrain water elev 0 with a circle just works on open grass.
- Elevated pond: 1) paint_terrain mountain elev N as a filled shape; 2) paint_terrain water elev N on INTERIOR cells only, a smaller shape inside it, keeping a ring of the mountain at least one cell wide; water above level 1 needs that mountain under it, so the mountain is a filled shape, never a rim alone. The ring at exactly elev N contains the water.
- Waterfall: water falls where a pool at level N meets ground one level or more lower across one row; the game draws the fall. Two ways, each legal by construction:
  a) carve_river begun inside a raised pool runs downhill and frames a fall at each step it crosses; two waypoints make a straight course. It is the way to bring water down several levels.
  b) One fall by hand: 1) the high ground at N; 2) the landing first, the row in front of the fall at one lower level across the fall's width and its two end cells; 3) the pool at N inside a ring of N; 4) paint water at N on the ring cells facing the landing, keeping a cell of mountain at exactly N at each end. Water painted down a slope by hand reverts.
- Hill with access: paint_terrain mountain elev 1 (rect/circle), then use find_ramp_sites to locate valid cliff-edge anchors and the matching ramp item, then place_object at a returned anchor — the heightDrop snap orients the ramp automatically. For elev 2+, build terraces (each tier >= {supportWindow} cells wider than the one above for V-MTN-03 headroom at >={supportFrom}) and call find_ramp_sites again for each tier.
- River + bridge: paint_terrain water elev 0 as a STRAIGHT line (width within the bridge's waterSpan in CATALOG) across grass — uniform width, flat equal banks. Then find_bridge_sites → place_object at a returned anchor. Round ponds usually have NO legal crossing (uneven span); give a pond a straight narrow waist if it needs a bridge.
- Road: lay_route joins two points across levels and water, placing the stair or bridge each needs; build_road lays an exact line or cell list on one level (roads need flat terrain under every cell).
- A whole place: draw_area with the ground, the paving and the planting as rows of text; mirror for matching sides, repeatX and repeatY for a repeated unit. One drawing replaces dozens of single calls and cannot be a cell off.
- Orchard or bed: scatter_objects with one species, pattern "grid" and step 2 for an orchard, pattern "fill" for a solid bed, over an exact rectangle; frame it with pattern "border" in a second species or with build_road around it. Repeat the finished block with copy_area.
- Symmetric pair: build one side (a yard, a bed, a pool, a terrace), then mirror_area it across the axis of the entrance or avenue: axisX is the path's middle column when the path is 1 or 3 wide, and that column plus 0.5 when it is 2 wide.
- Village block: find_flat_areas with the building's footprint (see CATALOG), place_object the buildings on one line at equal gaps facing the street, then connect doors with a road: build_road places each connecting line or cell sequence in one call. Give one house its yard (a border of one species, a bed or two inside), then copy_area that yard to the other houses.
