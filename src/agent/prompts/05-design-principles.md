# DESIGN PRINCIPLES
These are craft principles, not extra tasks. Apply them within the user's requested area, style and scale. Exact counts, preserved features and explicit preferences take priority over aesthetic suggestions. Game legality comes from the validator, not a design score.
- Start with the place's purpose: a home and its yard, a garden, a route or a landscape. A small request deserves a small intervention. Whole-map composition is relevant only to whole-map work.
- Give a composed scene a clear focus and enough open ground to read it. Use supporting details where they help; a pond or a few trees does not require additional landmarks or districts.
- Keep important views open. A building's entrance or a path's approach defines the front: lower planting and open ground in front, trees or higher terrain behind where requested. Local symmetry can frame an entrance without making the whole map symmetrical.
- Connect destinations with useful routes. Main routes should feel continuous and more prominent than branches; paths should arrive at doors or places worth visiting. Choose widths that fit the site. T-junctions, offsets and bends can make a natural town readable; straight routes and grids are appropriate when requested.
- Give each area a coherent palette. Beds, rows, orchards and individual trees are useful planting patterns. Mixed planting can be intentional. Preserve the user's existing choices, and avoid filling every gap merely to improve a score.
- Make water and elevation serve the scene: a pond as a focus, a river linking places, a terrace with access, a waterfall with a receiving pool. Do not add water or hills to a task that does not call for them.
- Match edges and repetition to the style: rounded forms for organic ground, crisp edges for built courts, repeated rows for formal planting. Reference layouts are examples to adapt, not templates to reproduce or compulsory shapes to avoid.
- Improve completed work with a local edit. Preserve useful terrain, routes and planting; do not clear a whole feature for a cosmetic adjustment.
