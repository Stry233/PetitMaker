GARDEN TOWN — the flat FORMAL dialect, measured from a hand-built expert map: order itself is the ornament. A REFERENCE SET PIECE: load it when the order asks for this look; on a free order its floorplan is never the default. Aligned repetition here IS the style: edging runs, orchard grids and twin courts are pattern, not stamps.

STAGE 0 — BOUND AND PARTITION (2-4 calls)
- BOUND THE TOWN: a composed district roughly 60-90 cells square around the plaza, NOT the whole island — the reference keeps open meadow outside its boundary streets, and a town stretched over everything is finished nowhere. Flat throughout.
- DRAW THE JUNCTION GRAMMAR, never hope for it: lay the 3-wide trunk first (build_road width 3, one call per straight run, ONE jog in its course, passing plaza + facility-shop + facility-station), then tee each 2-wide side street INTO it from ALTERNATING sides at staggered positions and STOP it at the trunk — a side street that continues across becomes the four-way that builds the grid. 4-6 panels of different sizes. update_plan the stages.
- THE SIZE LADDER is a placement law: a figure may OUTRANK the locked plaza (~20x27) only as the town's CROWN — centered on the plaza's own axis at the boundary (the reference's lake sits top-center straight above the plaza, 28-36 wide in a clearing that STAYS LAWN, and the two read as one composition). In a side panel or corner the figure is a SECONDARY: 10-14 wide, half the plaza or less — two co-equal masses side by side split the eye. Landmark plaza ~14; home courts ~10.

STAGE 1 — THE FIGURE AND THE CANAL (2-4 calls)
- ONE iconic figure where two streets frame it: draw_figure (heart, ring or crescent — symmetric by construction) or a composed mark via paint_terrain cells. Streets FRAME a figure, never cross it — stop the trunk at the clearing's edge (the reference's street ends at the water); a road through the figure cuts it into two crescents. It happens ONCE: any second water is a DIFFERENT shape, half the size, far away.
- AND ONE CANAL, always: a straight 3-4 wide ground-water line crossing the town, with the street running UP TO both banks and a bridge placed exactly at the crossing (find_bridge_sites, then place_object). Water stays NEAR the icon: one small accent pool (4x3) within a panel of the plaza — the reference frames its plaza with water on three sides, and a town whose only water is a corner ornament plus the canal reads water-poor.

STAGE 2 — THE COURTS (4-7 calls)
- EVERY home lives in a WALLED COURT — a bare cabin dropped on open grass is a defect, and if the town is full the fix is widening a court, never a box outside one. A court is an 8-12 cell panel, rim paved (2-wide) or hedged (shrub fill strip), 1-2 DISTINCT cabins facing a small green. The dooryard bed HUGS the rim or the cabin wall as a 1-2 wide strip — a free rectangle mid-lawn is a blob, not a bed. FINISH EACH COURT BEFORE THE NEXT. Exactly two courts MATCH (twins read as urban design); every other court gets a DIFFERENT interior program, not a variation — one is homes and a dooryard, one an orchard court, one a workshop yard, one a small pool court. Mirrored pairs are LOCAL: repeating the same mound or court on both axes turns the town into a kit of parts.
- One landmark stands alone on its own dirt/paved plaza ~10-14 across at the town's far corner (facility-pavilion or a remaining cabin), nothing else on it. The CENTRAL plaza gets the same respect on ALL FOUR faces: a 2-wide paved apron, then 3-4 cells of lawn — check each face before closing, since a ribbon or plot touching any one wall undoes the other three.

STAGE 3 — THE EDGING (3-5 calls)
- THE SIGNATURE: every street gets a 1-wide flower edge on BOTH sides — scatter_objects pattern "fill", one species per straight run, the rect EXACTLY a 1-cell strip (2-3 rows is a bed pretending). Edge a run END TO END or not at all; same for a figure\'s ring. ALTERNATE species run by run (rose, daisy, violet) — a planned ribbon, never per-cell mixture. No other flowers touch the streets.

STAGE 4 — THE PANELS (3-5 calls)
- One ORDERED block per panel, panels VARY (at most two orchards ON THE WHOLE MAP): an orchard is a COURT-BOUND patch, 10x6 to 16x8 with its own edge (scatter_objects pattern "grid" step 2-3, one tree species) — a lattice running past one panel is wallpaper, and the sweep will name it. Other panels: a crop panel (decorate_zone farm), a solid one-species flower field (pattern "fill"). One panel stays plain lawn on purpose. decorate_zone garden only inside a court.

DONE BAR: trunk visibly wider and jogged, side streets tee in, no four-way lattice; every street edged end to end; each court complete with its own dooryard bed, twins somewhere; ONE figure (a circle is not a figure) + the canal bridged at a street; landmark alone on its plaza; at least 2 spurs ENDING at a door; one empty panel. Then find_speckle and clear or replant every patch it names; view_map — the town should read as embroidery, pattern not noise.
